VLM-RS Audit: Source-Conditioned Cross-Task Transfer

Research question. When a remote-sensing vision-language model fails on one task, which parts of its learned understanding remain useful on another?

This study evaluates source-conditioned cross-task transfer in remote-sensing vision-language models. The goal is to separate genuinely reusable spatial understanding from task-specific behavior that may not survive a change in prompt, label space, or source imagery.

The audit is structured around controlled comparisons across tasks and data conditions. Rather than reporting a single aggregate score, the analysis focuses on where transfer succeeds, where it breaks, and whether failure patterns remain consistent across source conditions.

Methods and outputs:

  • Cross-task evaluation of remote-sensing vision-language models
  • Source-conditioned comparisons and controlled ablations
  • Regional and task-level error analysis
  • Visual summaries of transfer, failure, and retained capability

“When One Answer Breaks, What Survives? Source Conditioned Cross Task Transfer in Remote Sensing VLMs” — with G. S. Madhuri and S. Muhuri; in review at IEEE ICCCI 2026.

BEN-DG-10: Cross-Country Dissonance

Overview. BEN-DG-10 is a benchmark-led investigation of geographic domain shift. It examines how model behavior changes across countries, landscapes, and acquisition conditions, and where a strong average score can hide meaningful local failures.

The project treats geography as more than a background variable. Country-level differences in land cover, settlement form, seasonality, image quality, and sampling can produce performance gaps that are invisible when all regions are evaluated together.

Key analysis:

  • Cross-country benchmarking on geographically diverse data
  • Country-wise and regional performance disaggregation
  • Identification of domain-shift and data-quality failure modes
  • Comparison of robustness beyond global averages

“BEN-10: Cross-Country Dissonance” — with G. S. Madhuri and S. Muhuri; in review at IEEE SISIMPACT.

4D Blender Spatiotemporal Research

Can vision-language models read the sundial?

This ongoing project creates a controlled benchmark for time inference from overhead shadows. Two renders of the same static scene are generated at different times of day, allowing a model’s temporal reasoning to be studied without changes in object identity or scene geometry.

Blender makes it possible to control camera pose, lighting, sun position, object placement, and rendering conditions. By changing one factor at a time, the benchmark can test whether a model is reasoning from consistent geometric cues or relying on incidental visual patterns.

Planned workflow:

  • Construct controlled overhead scenes in Blender
  • Render matched scenes across different times and lighting conditions
  • Evaluate ordering, interval estimation, and time-of-day inference
  • Analyze sensitivity to object geometry, shadow length, and scene composition

This research is being conducted under the mentorship of Dr. Samya Muhuri at Thapar Institute of Engineering & Technology.

Spatio-temporal analysis of 4D structures

This companion research direction studies how spatial structure changes over time and how those changes can be represented, compared, and interpreted. The work uses Blender and BlenderGIS to develop controlled 4D scenes that connect geometric change with temporal reasoning.

Cross-Modality Medical Segmentation

This work addresses the challenge of learning reliable segmentations when labeled data are limited and image modalities differ. The proposed approach combines adaptive confidence-gated pseudo-labeling with patch-level consistency to select more trustworthy supervision during training.

The method moves beyond fixed confidence thresholds by allowing pseudo-label acceptance to adapt to the data and learning state. Patch consistency adds a local structural check, reducing the effect of unstable predictions and improving the quality of cross-modality supervision.

“Beyond Fixed Thresholds: Adaptive Confidence-Gated Pseudo-Labeling and Patch Consistency for Cross-Modality Medical Segmentation” — accepted at IPTA 2026; proceedings forthcoming.

Multi-Source Satellite Imagery Evaluation

At CNH Industrial, I am developing a multi-source satellite-imagery evaluation framework for agricultural field-boundary detection. The framework compares Sentinel-2, Google Maps, and OpenStreetMap tiles to determine which inputs are most reliable across geographically diverse agricultural regions.

The pipeline standardizes coordinate reference systems, spatial resolution, bounding boxes, cloud-cover information, revisit cadence, and ingestion quality. These controls support systematic cross-source benchmarking rather than ad hoc visual comparison.

Image-fusion experiments include pixel blending, pyramid fusion, and super-resolution. Each variant is evaluated against field-boundary model outputs using Intersection over Union, Dice score, and boundary precision and recall.

Earlier work benchmarked zero-shot and supervised segmentation approaches, including SAM, U-Net, and DeepLabV3+, across approximately 1,500 farms and three customer accounts. The analysis highlighted data-quality and geographic-variability risks affecting model robustness.
